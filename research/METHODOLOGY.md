NBA [terms of use](https://www.nba.com/termsofuse) do not provide blanket reuse rights for league imagery. Team colors and names are used to identify participants. All article summaries and story writing are original. No visitor request contacts a source site until the visitor chooses a citation link.

## Evidence rules

1. Prefer the completed team/league announcement over a proposal or breaking report. Cross-check dates against team transaction records and local calendar dates.
2. Record the entire announced package, including facilitation by additional teams, draft rights, cash and conditional obligations. Do not invent an exact cash amount or contract protection.
3. Cite sources on transactions and assets. `verified` means the recorded event is supported; it does not mean every contract term was made public. Unknown terms remain explicit.
4. An obligation has its own identity. Actual pick positions appear in a later event. A selection then becomes player draft rights through a second explicit conversion.
5. A swap right does not create a second first-round selection. Show the two actual allocations and the resolution of the right.
6. Reuse a canonical event across stories. Do not reconstruct a shared package differently for another perspective.
7. Stop at the declared coverage date. Never call an untraced endpoint a current holding or completed chain. No championships, awards or performance are treated as causal proof.

## Date and contract decisions

- Luka’s draft exchange: June 21, 2018 in the U.S.; NBA publication is June 22 UTC. Draft rights are distinguished from signed contracts.
- Dirk/Garrity and Nash: separate June 24, 1998 exchanges. Garrity’s draft rights connect the packages; the first exchange alone did not acquire Nash.
- Kobe: June 26 selection; July 11, 1996 rights trade from Charlotte’s official historical record.
- Butler/Kwame: completed August 2, 2005 sign-and-trade; the Wizards announcement has a July 29 publication date.
- Harden: Oklahoma City’s original release explicitly states October 27, 2012. Later reporting timestamps are not substituted for the announcement’s transaction date.
- Boston 2017: May 16 is the **lottery allocation date**, not an asserted administrative exercise filing date. The NBA’s published lottery result establishes Boston’s No. 1 allocation via Brooklyn. The scene notes this distinction. June 19 trade-down and June 22 selection are separate events. The accompanying Boston second-round obligation is included.
- Davis 2019: the Lakers first conveys at 1–8 in 2021, with 9–30 protected; otherwise 2022 is unprotected. Some recaps describe this imprecisely as top-eight protection. Use the precise ESPN account with the final package announcement.
- Davis 2026: final three-team structure, not the initially reported two-team proposal. The first is a composite least-favorable 2026 entitlement among OKC/HOU/LAC. The Golden State 2030 first has top-20 protection and a 2030 second-round fallback. No 2026 draft outcome is assigned.
- Holiday 2025: July 7 final exchange is Simons for Holiday. The two seconds in early reports were removed.
- Towns: Charlotte is an actual third participant. The 2025 second from Minnesota is a composite entitlement, not necessarily a Minnesota-origin pick. Sign-and-traded players and $7.2m cash are retained.
- Butler: use the final February 6 five-team package, with net endpoints for players routed through another participant. The NBA lists two second-round rights and cash to Golden State that some summaries omit. Unknown original franchises, years or protections for minor rights are explicitly undisclosed. No outcome is attached to those rights.
- Older Pau, Shaq, Dirk and Garnett obligations: where accessible records establish inclusion or actual conveyance but not a full original protection schedule, the missing schedule is not invented.
- Toronto’s reverse-lottery 2013 Harden obligation: only the verified 2013 4–14 conveyance range is enumerated; unused later ranges are not guessed.
- Miami’s obligation in the 2019 George package is preserved under original terms. Its separate 2022 amendment is outside this branch, so it is not displayed as an up-to-date live obligation.
